## 2. THE EXHIBITS (three receipts, elegant source lines)

**Section header:** Three things nobody prints on the box

**Exhibit 1 — The foam is theatre.**
Most mainstream toothpaste foams because of SLS — the same family of surfactant used in shampoo and washing-up liquid. The foam doesn't clean your teeth; brushing does. For some people, SLS may irritate the mouth and has been linked to recurring ulcers.¹
*¹ Systematic review, Journal of Oral Pathology & Medicine, 2019. SLS-free users reported fewer, shorter ulcers. Evidence mixed — we say "may" and mean it.*

**Exhibit 2 — The tube outlives you.**
A plastic toothpaste tube takes around 500 years to break down.² The UK bins roughly 300 million of them a year³ — and until 2025, they weren't even technically recyclable. They are now, in theory. In practice, "recyclable" only counts if every person rinses and returns theirs, forever. Aluminium doesn't need the asterisk: one material, infinitely recyclable, in the bin you already own.
*² Estimated degradation time for plastic laminate tubes in landfill. ³ British Dental Journal, 2021; UK industry moved to recyclable HDPE tubes in 2025 (WRAP, Recycle Week) — we're glad, and we still think the asterisk-free tube is better.*

**Exhibit 3 — You buy on autopilot.**
Nobody chooses toothpaste. You grab whatever's at eye level, run out at the worst moment, then spend three days squeezing a dead tube. The aisle counts on you never asking a single question about any of it.
